## 🎯 The Problem This Project Solves

> "Most sales reporting is built for spreadsheets, not for decisions — leadership has to dig through rows and tabs to find the one number that matters."

Across many sales teams, weekly performance tracking still looks like:

- ❌ Static Excel summaries that need manual updating every week
- ❌ Revenue and expense numbers sitting in separate sheets, never compared side by side
- ❌ Goal attainment calculated after the fact, not tracked in real time
- ❌ Top performers buried in a long unsorted list instead of ranked and visible
- ❌ No quick read on whether margin is improving or slipping month to month

This project is a working example of what a **clean, modern, business-ready dashboard** looks like when built with intent — every card answers one question, and nothing is on the page just to fill space.

## 💡 Key Insights

- Weekly Sales Goal attainment sits at **57%** — a visible gap for target-setting or coaching conversations
- Profit Margin ranged **16%–22%** across the year, with a dip in March worth investigating
- Sales consistently **peak mid-week (Tue/Wed)**, useful for timing promotions or staffing
- A small group of reps (Michael Brown, Jane Smith, Samantha Ray) repeatedly drive the highest-value deals
- Expense trends closely track revenue patterns, day over day

## ⚙️ Technical Highlights

- **Excel:** structured source tables, calculated fields for growth/percentage metrics
- **Tableau:** parameter-driven segment filters (Hardware / R&D / Software toggle with Boolean "Selected?" logic), dual-axis weekly Revenue vs. Expense bar charts, donut chart for Weekly Sales Goal, dumbbell-style circle chart for monthly Profit Margin trend, dynamic Top-N Sales table with customer logo icons
- **Design:** card-based layout, minimal color palette, consistent spacing — built to be read in seconds, not studied

## 🎓 Takeaway

A dashboard's job is to make the decision obvious, not to show off every chart type available. Pairing the right visual to the right KPI — a donut for a goal, a dumbbell for a trend, a ranked table for top performers — does more for clarity than adding more panels ever will.
